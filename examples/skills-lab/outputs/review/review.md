# Material review findings

The descriptive 60% versus 40% rates are correct. The causal claim, mandate and forecast are unsupported by the supplied observational cohort.

1. **Causality changes the decision.** Location: headline “Onboarding lifts retention by 50%” and “The new experience caused a 20-point lift.” Source counts give 60/100 versus 40/100, a 20 percentage-point gap and a 50% relative difference with noncompleters as denominator. Completion was chosen by users; no assigned treatment or older-experience comparator exists. Prior engagement or selection could produce the same observations. Countercheck: if there were random assignment to onboarding and suitable compliance/outcome handling, an intervention interpretation might be defensible, but none is supplied; even random assignment would not automatically justify comparing self-selected completers. Minimum revision: call this an observed association for this cohort, replace “caused”/“lifts” with descriptive wording, and place the self-selection limitation next to the claim. The data cannot establish whether onboarding helped, harmed, or had no effect.

2. **“Require every new user” exceeds the evidence.** Location: mandate recommendation. Completing voluntarily and being required to complete are different exposures; friction or other consequences of a mandate are unmeasured. Countercheck: a mandate might be worthwhile based on independent experiment or policy evidence, but none appears here. Minimum revision: recommend evaluating an assigned onboarding policy with week-four retention and completion/abandonment outcomes before adopting a universal requirement. Do not claim an experiment has run or that planning requires a mandate. PM can plan a test, not a proven retention improvement.

3. **“This will increase next month's retained users by half” uses an unsupported forecast and denominator.** Observed cohort totals are 100 retained of 200 eligible (50%). The 50% number compares 60% with 40% specifically; it is not uplift over the pooled 50% retention rate. Even an explicitly hypothetical world with the same 200 users all retaining at the observed completer rate gives 120 retained versus 100, a 20% increase, not 50%. This scenario is arithmetic only, not an estimate. Countercheck: a future cohort initially entirely at 40% could produce a 50% relative increase if a causal shift to 60% were established, but neither its composition nor that effect is supplied. Minimum revision: remove the next-month numerical forecast; mention absent future cohort size/mix and intervention evidence if a forecast is requested.

Presentation improvements: keep “week-four qualifying activity” and “one fully observed signup cohort” beside the rates, and distinguish percentage points from relative percent. These sharpen the already-correct group arithmetic; they do not fix identification by themselves.
