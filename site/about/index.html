Work with me

Data and analytics advisory from the former head of data for ESPN+. Analytics judgment for your team and the agents it uses.

I'm Patrick Morris. I ran data for ESPN+, leading the analytics organization as the service grew to 25 million subscribers. Before that I built the experimentation platform at The New York Times. I work with a small number of heads of data, analytics, and product on the decisions that run on their numbers.

I built aftergrid because agents got good at fetching the number before they got good at the work that follows. The answers reaching product and growth decisions were fast and confident. They reported a rate without sizing it against the goal. Some were simply wrong in ways an experienced analyst would catch in a minute, like recommending a rollback because of a traffic shift, or celebrating revenue growth that hid churn.

The skills are free and MIT. Consulting is how I help directly.

Built the unified metrics framework behind billion-dollar sports rights evaluations, and a self-serve reporting suite that cut ad-hoc requests by 60%.

Consulting

Engagements for VPs and heads of data, analytics, and product. Each one starts with the decisions your leadership keeps re-litigating.

  • Analytics leadership advisory. A senior sounding board for the person who owns the numbers: metric strategy, operating model, team design, and hiring.
  • Metrics and decision review. Choose the metrics that matter and define them so a second analyst gets the same number: numerator, denominator, eligibility, time rules, counter-metrics. Then review the analyses reaching leadership against the goals they serve.
  • Experimentation programs. Design or reset a testing program people trust: platform choices, guardrail metrics, a review step, and a cadence that gets from a handful of tests to a hundred a month.
  • Analytics agents your team can trust. Put the aftergrid practice into the agents your team already uses. make-the-call states results against your targets, analysis-review checks numbers before they reach a decision, and a capture-and-retrieval loop means the fourth analyst does not start from the same trap.

Your data stays in your repository. I do not approve your metrics; the owner does. With your consent, each engagement contributes one sanitized observation to the public examples.
