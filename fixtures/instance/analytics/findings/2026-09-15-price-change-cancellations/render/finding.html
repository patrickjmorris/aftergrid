Too early to tell whether the price change made more people cancel

For: Non-technical product owner · From: Dana Okafor · Generated 2026-09-15T09:30:00Z · Revision 1

Answer

We cannot tell yet. Only 7 days of data exist since the price change, and the team's own rule is to wait for 28 days and 300 cancellations before comparing.

The one thing to keep in mind

This is not evidence that the price change is fine. It is evidence that we cannot tell yet. The day count is met once 2026-10-05 has closed; an answer also needs 300 cancellations and an approved definition, so it may still be too early then.

Decision it informs

Whether to keep the new price, roll it back, or wait. Dana Okafor owns the decision. This analysis cannot choose between keeping and rolling back; Dana Okafor decides whether to wait or to use other evidence.

Where the minimum comes from

The team's analysis policy (dated 2026-03-10, owned by Dana Okafor) requires at least 28 full days after a change, so one billing cycle is covered, and at least 300 cancellations, so a change of a fifth can be told apart from normal week-to-week noise. Query cancellations_since_change; saved result since_change. The earliest window end is the change date plus the policy day minimum, computed from the same numbers, as is the minimum-met flag.

Why there is no comparison yet

The cancellations so far are not compared with the weeks before the change, because the first days after a price change mix people reacting to the announcement with normal cancellations.

A partial first week set against full earlier weeks would compare unlike things and invite a conclusion the data cannot support. No daily series is shown here, so this Finding says nothing about whether cancellations spiked in the first days; that would need its own evidenced claim.

Limits of this claim
  • This is a method choice. When the minimum is met, the full before-and-after comparison replaces it.

What would change our mind

Once 28 days and 300 cancellations have accumulated, the belief that the price change did not raise cancellations is wrong if the daily cancellation rate after the change is more than a fifth higher than in the 28 days before it. That test is written down and waiting.

On a re-check with newer data, the first claim is re-tested by restating whether both policy minimums are met on the same population; only once both are met does it stop holding, and this Finding should be redone with the full comparison. The second claim is a method choice, not a measurement; Dana Okafor decides when to retire it.
