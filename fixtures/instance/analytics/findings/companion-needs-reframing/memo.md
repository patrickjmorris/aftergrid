# Whether podcast listeners are happier cannot be answered from our data

## Answer

The question cannot be answered as asked. Loop keeps no measure of how happy a user is, so nothing in the data can say whether podcast listeners are happier.

<!-- material_caveat -->
Listeners may well be happier. Nothing here says they are not; it says we have no way to tell from the data we keep.

## Decision it informs

Whether to invest the next quarter in the podcast feature. Dana owns the decision. A question we could answer instead: do people who play podcasts keep using Loop longer than people who do not? That uses data we have (app opens and subscriptions), though it measures staying, not happiness.

## Evidence

### Loop's data contains no measure of how happy a user is, so the question cannot be answered as asked. <!-- claim: c1 -->

Who is counted: all Loop users. What was looked at: the list of tables and columns in the warehouse. There is no survey, rating, review or support signal among them. A satisfaction signal might exist in a tool the warehouse does not load, such as app-store reviews or support tickets; that is a question for Dana, not something the data can settle.

## How we checked

No Checks ran because no numbers were produced. The warehouse catalog was inspected on the fourteenth of September. No definition is cited. This is a draft and has not been approved for publication.

## What would change our mind

No test can be written for the question as asked, because there is no observation in the data that could show the belief wrong; inventing one would be a fiction. This Finding changes only if a satisfaction measure is added to the warehouse; Dana decides whether to add one or to reframe the question.
