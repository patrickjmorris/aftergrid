# A merged Finding is a standing test of the decision it informed

Every source we studied says the moat is the feedback loop, and every one of them tests the agent, not the decisions. Analysts meanwhile lose a week whenever someone asks "is that analysis still true?" We make the "What would change our mind" section executable. Each Claim carries a tolerance and each Question carries a machine-checkable falsifier; a Decision record carries a revisit condition. `aftergrid revisit` refreshes the Snapshot, reruns the saved SQL, compares every Claim to its reviewed value within tolerance, evaluates the falsifier as a Check, and produces a new Finding revision whose Answer is one of: the decision still holds, the decision rests on a Claim that flipped, or the data can no longer support the Claim. The Decision log becomes a live board of which beliefs still hold. This is golden-Question machinery pointed at a team's own past Findings, so the cost is two manifest fields in v0 and a command in v0.1; without the fields in v0, no v0 Finding could ever be revisited. Alternatives rejected: leaving refresh manual (the week-long project this replaces), or alerting on raw metric thresholds (which tests numbers, not the decisions that depend on them). A flip on thin data is noise, so the same minimum-data and tolerance rules as golden Questions apply, and a revisit always yields a revision for Operator review, never an automatic verdict.


## Amended 2026-09-15, after independent review

Tolerance from an old value is the wrong test. "Conversion exceeds 10%" still holds when it moves from 12% to 20% though a small tolerance is exceeded, and 10.1% to 9.9% sits inside a tolerance while crossing the threshold the decision rests on. So each Claim carries a **Recheck policy**: the proposition (predicate, threshold, direction), the comparison method or a versioned method reference, the evidence it reads, tolerance units and baseline where they apply, minimum-data and coverage requirements, and a window policy (fixed-window restatement or advancing-window comparison); or an explicit not-automatically-evaluable state with a reason. Associational and causal Claims are never reduced to scalar deltas; they require their identification assumptions and method to remain applicable, otherwise the result is *not comparable, method review required*. A Recheck returns holds, contradicted, insufficient data or not comparable; these are results, distinct from engine or data-quality Check failures, and a contradicted falsifier is a valid, reviewable Finding, not a failed gate. The Engine never infers "decision holds": each Decision record names the Claims it rests on and its own revisit condition, and the owner judges. A Revisit's refresh creates new retained input ids and a new immutable revision, so prior replay and rerun guarantees are untouched; population or definition changes since the original make the comparison not comparable. v0 validates the shape and references of these fields; the evaluator is v0.1.
